New to buy-to-let? Start here
Before you make an offer
  • Get a financing pre-approval (Finanzierungsbestätigung) — sellers and agents expect one.
  • Budget 10–15% on top of the price for purchase costs (agent, notary, transfer tax) — you'll usually need this in cash, banks rarely finance it.
  • Keep a cash buffer beyond the deposit: at least 3–6 months of mortgage + costs, for vacancy or repairs.
  • For condos (Eigentumswohnung): request the last 3 years of Hausgeldabrechnung, the Teilungserklärung, and minutes of owner meetings (Eigentümerversammlung) — this reveals hidden maintenance backlogs.
  • Order or review the Energieausweis (energy certificate) — affects future retrofit obligations and tenant appeal.
Common first-time mistakes
  • Chasing high gross yield in a weak/shrinking location — vacancy risk usually costs more than the extra yield is worth.
  • Underestimating the maintenance reserve (Instandhaltungsrücklage) — old buildings need it more than the Hausgeld statement suggests.
  • Ignoring the WEG's reserve fund (Rücklage) balance — a low one means a special assessment (Sonderumlage) could hit you soon after buying.
  • Not stress-testing the mortgage rate — check the "Bank meeting" tab's rate-sensitivity line before you sign a rate lock.
  • Treating projected rent growth as guaranteed — model a flat-rent scenario too (this tool defaults to flat for that reason).
Reading this tool
  • Green/yellow/red KPI colors are rough guides, not pass/fail — a "red" cashflow can still be fine if you have income to cover it and expect rent/value growth.
  • The 30-year projection is a scenario, not a forecast — rerun it with flat rent, higher rates and no appreciation to see your downside case.
  • Use the Scenarios tab to save 2–3 versions of the same deal (e.g. different financing) before deciding.
  • This tool does not replace a tax advisor, especially for the depreciation and capex-capitalization rules — those decisions have real audit consequences.
